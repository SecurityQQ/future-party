Twenty-nine tracks and five spoken voices about the future, two hours in all, laid out in the three blocks of the projection. Every track earns its place by being grounded in reality: it played on a real mission, it was made with a technology that will be ordinary by 2050, or its prediction has already come true.     How to play it

    A list to import into your player

    1. The simplest way: play the YouTube playlist, which has all 34 items in order, voices included.
    2. For Spotify, Apple Music or Yandex Music, copy the list: every track in running order, without the voices. Russian songs stay in Cyrillic so streaming search finds them.
    3. Paste it into TuneMyMusic, which turns a text list into a playlist on Spotify, Apple Music or Yandex Music, or into Yandex Music’s import page.
    4. Play the voices from YouTube between tracks. Moroder’s monologue is the start of the Daft Punk track, so it is on streaming. Wanderers plays with its own sound.
    5. Set a 5–8 second crossfade, and turn it off for the voices.

      What was not checked

      Where this could be wrong

      • Scores are editorial. Plausibility here means a link to a real mission, a technology or a prediction that came true, not a guess at how music will sound in 2050.
      • Eno’s words about the “sound of the future” are known only from Bowie’s account (1989), and the second half of the quote varies between sources.
      • Clarke’s and Asimov’s words are paraphrased; the exact wording is in the linked recordings.
      • Some sources call “Trava u doma” the official anthem of Russian cosmonautics since 2009, others an unofficial one. I found no document on its status.
      • The Mars biosignature is only potential: a non-biological explanation is not ruled out, and the sample has to come to Earth.
      • Durations come from the Apple Music catalog, and other services can differ by a few seconds. Durations of the voices and of “Prekrasnoye daleko” are estimates.
      • The history is a selection weighted toward Western and recorded music. Early dates are approximate, and Pythagoras’s discovery is a tradition, not a documented fact.
      • “First” claims, such as the first jazz record or the first electric guitar, follow Wikipedia and are debated by historians.
      • The 2050 forecasts extrapolate from the history; they are not predictions from a model or a survey.
      • Listen links go to a search, not to a specific version of the track.
