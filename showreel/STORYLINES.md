# Six Facts: three storylines

The brief: one show reel that combines the predictions from *Further*, quotes from the books, quotes from X
and epic footage, around "Six facts about how books guess the future". Storyline A was built. B and C are
kept here so a later turn can switch.

## A. Six facts, cut to the score (built)

- **Device and form.** A number lands, six times. Cut on the bar of an orchestral score.
- **What the viewer sees.** Each fact arrives as a giant number over real footage, then a book answers it,
  then someone on X answers the book, then two predictions from *Further* show how far it goes. Six
  chapters, each the same four voices in an order the music picks.
- **First two seconds.** Night at Kennedy Space Center. The title is already on screen and the horizon
  behind it catches fire as Artemis I lifts off.
- **Beats.** title → 71% → 1972 → 8.1 vs 10.3 → 119 Gt → 38 → 86% vs 7% → "Every future starts here" → 2050.
- **Look.** The wall films' look (ground #0b0b0d, one accent #ff5315, League Gothic and Inter) over NASA
  footage, with the book serif for quotes.
- **Sound.** "Call to Adventure" at 86 BPM: quiet opening, first theme at chapter 2, climax under
  chapter 6, last chord on the last slide. A low impact on each fact, a short whoosh into each pair of slides.

## B. The forecast, checked

- **Device and form.** The object is the explanation: one ruler of years from 1972 to 2050. A flight
  through one world, no cuts.
- **What the viewer sees.** The camera travels along a single line of years. Every book, tweet and
  prediction hangs on the year it was written and points at the year it talks about, so the viewer watches
  forecasts overshoot, undershoot and land.
- **First two seconds.** The line draws from 1972 and the camera is already moving.
- **Beats.** 1972 → 2012 → 2020 → 2026 (now, the line turns from ink to accent) → 2035 → 2050.
- **Look.** Ground, one hairline in ink, footage only inside round windows on the line.
- **Sound.** A steady pulse that drops out at "now" and returns a fifth higher.

## C. Who said it? (the bold one)

- **Device and form.** A question with the input. One frame that rebuilds.
- **What the viewer sees.** A line appears with no name: is it a book, a tweet or a film? The room guesses
  out loud, the frame rebuilds into the answer, and a fact plays as the answer card between rounds.
- **First two seconds.** "We will extend our minds many millions-fold by 2045." Three doors: BOOK, TWEET, FILM.
- **Beats.** line → three doors → reveal → fact, six rounds, the last answer is "you".
- **Look.** Three flat colour fields, one per kind of source, type only.
- **Sound.** A game-show tick under each question, a single chord on each reveal.
