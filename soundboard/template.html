      Play 2050 · setup

      Wire up the Makey Makey

      Set it up in five minutes

      1. Plug the Makey Makey into this computer with its USB cable. It shows up as a keyboard and a mouse, so there is nothing to install.
      2. Clip one alligator clip to EARTH, the strip along the bottom of the board. Whoever plays holds the metal end of that clip.
      3. Clip wires to the arrows, SPACE and CLICK, and the other ends to anything a little conductive: bananas, foil, Play-Doh, spoons, a cup of water, a thick pencil drawing.
      4. For six more pads, push jumper wires into W A S D F G on the back of the board.
      5. Hold EARTH and touch a banana. Press Enter for stage mode on the projector.

      Jam mode: why it always sounds good

      • One tempo, one key, one chord loop per kit. Every kit has a backing beat with a bass line and chords that follow a four-chord loop, so every sound has something to sit on.
      • Snap to beat. A touch waits for the next sixteenth note (loops wait for the next beat), so hits land in time even when the banana is late.
      • Auto-tune. Every sample's pitch is measured when it loads and moved onto the chord that is playing. Drums and noises are left as they are.
      • Pads with a job. Melody pads play a short tune on a pentatonic scale, which has no clashing notes. The tune depends on where in the bar you touch, so tapping a rhythm plays the same hook every two bars. Arpeggio pads climb the chord, bass pads play its root, chord pads play all three notes.
      • Contour keys. In kit 8, Magic Keys, the back row W A S D F G is one horn section: a pin to the right of the last one goes up, to the left goes down, the same pin repeats. Lay the bananas out in that order.
      • The beat follows the room. Every touch adds energy and quiet lets it drain. Every four bars the beat picks a level (intro, groove, full, peak) and announces a change with a drum fill and a crash. After a long run at peak it breaks down, builds up and drops. When nobody plays for a while it thins out, stops, and leaves soft chimes until the next touch.
      • Harmony. At full and peak, lead notes get a softer second voice a third below.
      • Loops in time. Breaks like the Amen are stretched to the tempo and lined up with the bar, wherever you come in.
      • A produced mix. The kick pumps the chords, every touch ducks the backing and the reverb a little, the echo repeats on a dotted eighth, drums swing where the style calls for it, and touches on the beat are played a little harder than those between.

      Kit 7, Jam Session, is built for playing music: lead, bass, chords, arpeggio and strum, with a riser that drops on the next bar. Press J to turn it all off and play sounds exactly as recorded.

      The sky follows the music too. Each pin has its own animation: a comet, a meteor, orbits, a star bursting, a galaxy, a constellation, a radio signal, a pulsar, a field of lights or an aurora. The planet's rim glows in the colour of the chord and the kick brightens it, the stars drift faster as the room gets busier, and a drop sends them into warp.

      Party ideas

      • High-five circuit. One guest holds EARTH, another touches a pad. Nothing plays until they high-five. A whole chain of people holding hands works too.
      • Fruit console. Six bananas on the front pins, the kit on the projector, a queue forms.
      • Foil dance floor. Tape foil squares to the floor and load Rave 2050. Bare feet conduct; shoes don't.
      • Pencil pads. Draw thick, filled shapes with a soft pencil (6B or softer) and clip onto the drawing.
      • Robot greeter. Put the Robot Choir announcer on the door handle.

      If a pin stays silent

      • The player has to touch bare metal on the EARTH clip. The small light on the Makey Makey shows when a pin closes.
      • Holding a pin repeats nothing: each touch is one hit, and Play while held pads stop when you let go.
      • If your Makey Makey was reprogrammed to other keys, use Remap pins and touch each pin once.
