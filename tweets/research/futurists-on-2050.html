Every quote about 2050 found from 12 futurists and technologists on X, word for word, with a link to each original. Many quotes are goals or conditions rather than forecasts, so each carries a label. The quotes say almost nothing about birth rates, geopolitics, war, democracy, money, AI risk, water or longevity; Missing topics shows what X has on 19 such gaps.

    What to keep in mind

    • The choice of “best futurists” is mine: well-known people who think about the long-term future for a living or build the technology, and who had tweets about 2050 specifically. Follower counts show reach, not accuracy.
    • Many quotes are not forecasts. Khosla calls his list “the world we can create”; Musk writes “we need to”; Harari and Ritchie write “if”. Diamandis often cites UN figures or summarises Kurzweil. The labels mark this.
    • Consensus, topics and ideas are my grouping. Each quote sits under one main topic, though some touch several. The consensus threshold is three authors.
    • Not found: tweets about 2050 by Jane McGonigal, Tomas Pueyo or Sam Altman (Altman appears only through Diamandis). Ray Kurzweil's own tweets did not come up; only Diamandis's summary did. The first search also missed Amy Webb and Gerd Leonhard, partly because it used the wrong handle for Leonhard (he posts as @gleonhard); the missing-topics search found a few of their 2050 tweets, described under Missing topics.
    • Russian-language X had no substantive futurist predictions for 2050, only the UN, the media and stray posts.
    • How the text is shown. Text and counts come straight from the X API. Links that X shortens (t.co) appear as [link]; the @handles that open a reply are moved to a “Replying to” line. Views are missing for tweets from before X began counting them.
    • Search coverage. X search through the API (Top and Latest) can miss older or less visible tweets.
    • Missing topics were searched on 13 November 2026 by three search agents working in parallel, each with its own list of topics and accounts. I checked every quote shown there against the raw X responses and dropped weak ones: a polemic, a hype thread, a paper title, a joke, and accounts whose bios show no relevant role. “Nothing found” means these searches found nothing usable, not that nobody has tweeted about it.
