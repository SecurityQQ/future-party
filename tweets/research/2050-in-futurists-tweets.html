What 2050 looks like
to futurists on X

Put together, the most prominent futurists and technologists on X say that by 2050 expertise and labour will be almost free (AI doctors, AI tutors, a billion robots), energy will be mostly clean, the population will be older, and the internet will be used mostly by AI agents. The most detailed picture is Vinod Khosla’s 14 predictions (January 2026). Many statements are conditional (“could”, “if we have time”), so each one carries a label.

14

Khosla has the most detailed list

Fourteen predictions for 2050: near-free AI doctors and AI tutors, a billion robots, more than a billion people “programming” in plain language, tens of billions of agents on the internet, fusion boilers in place of coal and gas ones, Mach 5 planes. He calls it “the world we can create”.

2–3×

More energy will be needed, and it will be cleaner

Ramez Naam expects global electricity demand to grow two to three times by 2050. Diamandis agrees with Sam Altman that by 2050 “we won’t be burning many fossil fuels”. Khosla bets on fusion boilers.

To the tweets ↓
≈2030

Strong AI arrives long before 2050

Daniel Kokotajlo moved his AGI forecast “from ~2050 to 2030”. Diamandis relays Kurzweil: AGI in 2029, the singularity in the 2040s. Khosla: “Maybe 80% of 80% of all jobs by 2050 or much earlier?”

To the tweets ↓
1 in 5

The world gets older

Musk: the real issue by 2050 will be an “aging & declining world population”, not overpopulation. Diamandis: by 2050 one person in five will be over 60. Kevin Kelly quotes The Economist: 2.1 billion grandparents, 22% of humanity.

To the tweets ↓
~2050

A city on Mars, under conditions

Musk hopes a self-sustaining city on Mars is built “by ~2050”: ten years of making 800 to 1,000 Raptor engines a year, then about twenty years to build the city itself.

Life and society

Kelly: by 2050 the web becomes “an ever-present type of conversation”. Balaji: we may no longer know what distant people look like. Noah Smith: America will be “a really nice country”. Harari: a carbon-neutral economy by 2050 if 2% of world GDP is invested every year.

Limits

What to keep in mind

  • The choice of “best futurists” is an editorial one: well-known people who think about the long-term future for a living or build the technology, and who had tweets about 2050 specifically. Follower counts show reach, not how accurate anyone’s forecasts are.
  • Many statements are not forecasts in the strict sense. Khosla calls his list “the world we can create”; Musk writes “we need to” about the transport fleet; Harari and Ritchie write “if”. Diamandis often cites UN figures or summarises Kurzweil. Each tweet is labelled by type.
  • No tweets about 2050 were found from Jane McGonigal, Tomas Pueyo or Sam Altman (Altman appears only through Diamandis). Kurzweil’s own tweets did not come up, only Diamandis’s summary of them. The first search missed Amy Webb and Gerd Leonhard, partly because it used the wrong handle for Leonhard (he posts as @gleonhard). A second search on 13 November found a few of their 2050 tweets: a warning about AGI risk from Leonhard, and from Webb a question about someone else’s book and a thought about the president of 2050. They are covered in the Missing topics section of Futurists on 2050.
  • Russian-language X had no substantive futurist predictions for 2050, only the UN, the media and stray posts.
  • Search coverage. X search through the API (Top and Latest) can miss older or less visible tweets. Texts and figures come straight from the API responses.
  • Khosla’s list was posted on X on 5 January 2026, but by his own account he made it “two years ago at TED and at our CEO conference”.
